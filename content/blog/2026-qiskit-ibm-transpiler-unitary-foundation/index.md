🏆 Three years in a row!

For the third consecutive year, [`qiskit-ibm-transpiler`](https://github.com/Qiskit/qiskit-ibm-transpiler/) is among the 5 most used libraries in the *Full-stack development platforms, compilers, and simulators* category of the [Unitary Foundation's 2026 Quantum Open Source Survey](https://unitaryfoundation.github.io/survey-2026/#Full-stack-development). This year it ranks #4, right after Qiskit, Qiskit Aer and PennyLane, and ahead of the Classiq library.

The library went public in late 2023. That means it has been in the top 5 of its category every year since its release: [#4 in 2024](/blog/2024-qiskit-ibm-transpiler-unitary-fund-survey/), [#3 in 2025](/blog/2025-qiskit-ibm-transpiler-unitary-foundation/), and #4 in 2026. As of today, it has been downloaded about [990K times from PyPI](https://pepy.tech/projects/qiskit-ibm-transpiler).

What it does: `qiskit-ibm-transpiler` brings the [AI-powered transpiler passes](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/qiskit/ai-transpiler-passes) to Qiskit users. These passes deliver state-of-the-art results in transpilation and synthesis for many different kinds of circuits. The method behind them is described in our paper ["Practical and efficient quantum circuit synthesis and transpiling with Reinforcement Learning"](/publications/2024-synthesisrl/) ([arXiv:2405.13196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13196)).

Our team is now moving away from maintaining and developing the library, which makes this recognition feel even more special. It reflects work done over several years by a great group of people.

Kudos to everyone involved: David Kremer, Víctor Villar Pascual, Jesús Talavera Gómez, Yaiza García Martín-Mantero, and the other former IBMers who contributed along the way. Thank you, too, to the community for using the library and sharing your feedback.
